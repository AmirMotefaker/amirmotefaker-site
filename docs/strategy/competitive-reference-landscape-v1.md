## Purpose

Translate the market baseline and audience decision model into a concrete reference landscape for AmirMotefaker.ir.

The goal is **not** to copy a founder website. The goal is to identify which patterns reduce decision uncertainty for Amir's audiences and which patterns would weaken a multi-product master brand.

This landscape evaluates twelve current founder-led websites across nine dimensions:

1. founder clarity;
2. portfolio coherence;
3. evidence quality;
4. editorial authority;
5. investor / strategic usefulness;
6. international / multilingual usefulness;
7. SEO / entity clarity;
8. visual distinctiveness;
9. conversion design.

Scores are directional design-research judgments, not objective performance measurements. A high score means the site demonstrates a pattern useful to this project; it does not mean the site should be imitated visually.

## Scoring scale

| Score | Interpretation |

## Reference set

### R1 — Patrick Collison

**Reference:** <https://patrickcollison.com/>

The homepage behaves as a compact index into interests, questions, research, advice, books, labs and dispatches. The strongest transferable pattern is the visible **knowledge graph of the founder's mind** rather than a conventional personal-brand funnel.

**Useful for Amir:** Thesis/Notes architecture, intellectual range, durable internal linking, low-noise navigation.

**Do not copy:** extreme minimalism would under-serve Amir's product user and partner journeys because AmirMotefaker.ir must explain multiple active products and availability states.

### R2 — Pieter Levels

**Reference:** <https://levels.io/projects/>

Levels exposes a large project history with status, dates, outcomes and explicit failures. The project index functions simultaneously as portfolio, operating history and evidence of shipping frequency.

**Useful for Amir:** lifecycle transparency, project chronology, explicit status, proof through shipped artifacts, honest separation of success/failure/new work.

**Do not copy:** an exhaustive maker log would make Amir's portfolio harder to evaluate unless projects are classified into active portfolio, legacy, experiments and archived work.

### R3 — Sahil Lavingia

**Reference:** <https://sahillavingia.com/>

The site compresses identity into a small set of roles—founder, writer and painter—and then lets authored work become the primary evidence layer.

**Useful for Amir:** concise role architecture, authored material as proof, strong founder voice.

**Do not copy:** the sparse product context is insufficient for a master brand whose job includes routing users to several distinct products.

### R4 — Matt Mullenweg

**Reference:** <https://ma.tt/> and <https://ma.tt/about/>

The About page connects personal identity, WordPress, Automattic, products, investment activity and long-running authored work. The site demonstrates how a founder can carry a large historical footprint without collapsing all activity into a corporate page.

**Useful for Amir:** long-horizon founder narrative, explicit relationship between founder and products, durable archive, category-specific authored content.

**Do not copy:** blog chronology should not become AmirMotefaker.ir's homepage logic; the master site's primary job is portfolio understanding and decision routing.

### R5 — Jason Fried

**Reference:** <https://world.hey.com/jason>

Jason's writing hub opens with a clear identity statement tied directly to 37signals, Basecamp and HEY, then makes his first-hand thinking the reason to return.

**Useful for Amir:** short founder-product relationship statement, first-hand editorial authority, recurring subscriber/reader relationship.

**Do not copy:** a writing-first homepage would hide the portfolio and produce weak product discovery for Amir's current stage.

### R6 — David Heinemeier Hansson

**Reference:** <https://dhh.dk/>

DHH's homepage is unusually explicit about distinct roles and evidence: creator of Rails and Omarchy, co-owner of 37signals, author, investor, board member and racing history. Major claims are supported through named products, organizations, books and public outcomes.

**Useful for Amir:** evidence-dense founder summary, role segmentation, proof attached to claims, clear product/company relationships.

**Do not copy:** the long one-page biography is useful as an About reference but would be too broad for Amir's Home decision hierarchy.

### R7 — Paul Graham

**Reference:** <https://www.paulgraham.com/> and <https://www.paulgraham.com/articles.html>

Paul Graham's site is dominated by a durable essay archive. Its authority comes from a long-running corpus, stable URLs and a direct connection between biography, operating history and ideas.

**Useful for Amir:** canonical long-form archive, topic authority, durable article URLs, first-person intellectual record.

**Do not copy:** the visual and navigational model is intentionally old-web and does not satisfy Amir's need for product-state comparison, bilingual IA and structured conversion routes.

### R8 — Nat Friedman

**Reference:** <https://nat.org/>

Nat's site explains identity through a concise personal fact set followed by an unusually clear set of beliefs. It makes worldview explicit instead of leaving visitors to infer it from scattered posts.

**Useful for Amir:** Thesis architecture, compact founder facts, strong separation between `who I am` and `what I believe`.

**Do not copy:** investor, product-user and partner actions are not the primary job of the reference site.

### R9 — Derek Sivers

**Reference:** <https://sive.rs/> and <https://sive.rs/about>

Sivers uses layered identity: `me in 10 seconds`, a deeper About page, a Now page, articles, books, projects and direct contact. The site explicitly describes itself as the master source for his work.

**Useful for Amir:** progressive disclosure, canonical-source mindset, compact-to-deep founder narrative, strong internal content graph, human contact clarity.

**Do not copy:** Amir needs qualified intent routing rather than one universal direct-contact path.

### R10 — Kevin Rose

**Reference:** <https://www.kevinrose.com/about>

The About experience provides a structured professional biography spanning products, investing, media, recognition and current focus.

**Useful for Amir:** media-ready fact base, timeline/role clarity, approved profile asset pattern, recognition separated from primary identity.

**Do not copy:** a recognition-heavy biography should not outrank current product truth or evidence of active work.

### R11 — Sam Altman

**Reference:** <https://blog.samaltman.com/>

The site is primarily a first-person writing archive. Its value as a reference is the directness of founder-authored thinking and durable public statements over time.

**Useful for Amir:** founder voice, primary-source notes, dated intellectual history.

**Do not copy:** the site does not function as a multi-product portfolio router and therefore cannot serve as Amir's structural model.

### R12 — Dharmesh Shah

**Reference:** <https://dharmesh.com/>

Dharmesh's site connects a crisp founder/CTO identity with mission, content, projects and specialized routes such as speaking information. The current homepage explicitly frames products and content as mechanisms for one personal mission.

**Useful for Amir:** master-brand mission connected to products, content and relationships; media/speaking utility; product-maker identity.

**Do not copy:** generic project placeholders or broad mission language should not substitute for Amir's product-level lifecycle and proof requirements.

### Reading the scores

The total score is less important than the pattern by dimension.

No single reference solves AmirMotefaker.ir's combined problem. The highest-value patterns are distributed across several sites:

- **DHH** demonstrates the strongest integration of founder identity, products, roles and explicit evidence.
- **Pieter Levels** demonstrates unusually strong project-state transparency and proof-through-shipping.
- **Derek Sivers** demonstrates canonical ownership, progressive disclosure and a coherent content graph.
- **Patrick Collison** and **Nat Friedman** are strong references for a visible intellectual / thesis layer.
- **Jason Fried**, **Paul Graham** and **Sam Altman** show the compounding value of first-person authored archives.
- **Matt Mullenweg** demonstrates how a long-running founder entity can connect historical activity, current operating roles and a large content archive.
- **Kevin Rose** is useful for media-ready founder evidence and structured career proof.
- **Dharmesh Shah** is useful for connecting personal mission, product creation, content and specialized external inquiries.

## Landscape gaps Amir can exploit

### Gap 1 — Portfolio truth is usually weaker than founder storytelling

Many founder sites are excellent at biography or writing but weak at answering:

- which products are active now;
- which are experiments or legacy;
- who each product is for;
- what is available today;
- what evidence supports each product claim.

**Amir opportunity:** make lifecycle and evidence state first-class portfolio data.

### Gap 2 — Product portfolios often hide the founder's decision logic

Corporate portfolio pages usually show logos, categories and marketing copy but not why the portfolio exists or what operating thesis connects the products.

**Amir opportunity:** explicitly connect `Founder → Thesis → Products → Proof`.

### Gap 3 — Founder sites rarely optimize for multiple decision audiences

Most references assume one dominant behavior: read, subscribe, contact or inspect projects.

Amir has at least four important human decisions:

- investor / capital evaluation;
- strategic partnership fit;
- product user / buyer fit;
- talent / specialist collaboration fit.

**Amir opportunity:** use audience-aware routes without turning the homepage into four separate funnels.

### Gap 4 — Persian-first bilingual founder portfolios are not represented in the reference set

The global references are overwhelmingly English-first. Translation alone will not solve Amir's requirement.

**Amir opportunity:** treat Persian as the primary semantic experience while maintaining English pages that remain independently useful to partners, media, search and international evaluators.

### Gap 5 — Structured evidence is mostly implicit

High-quality founder sites often contain excellent evidence, but the trust model is implicit rather than formally represented.

**Amir opportunity:** store evidence level, source, review date and lifecycle state in the canonical content model so public claims can be governed rather than manually remembered.

## Design principles derived from the landscape

### P1 — Identity in one screen

A qualified visitor should determine within one screen:

- Amir Motefaker is a technology founder and product builder;
- he operates a portfolio of independent technology products;
- the site is the canonical source for his work, products and thinking;
- there is a clear path to products, proof and the relevant conversation.

### P2 — Product status must be visible

Every portfolio card and product page should expose an approved lifecycle state such as:

- Live;
- Beta;
- In development;
- Research / validation;
- Legacy / archived.

Avoid ambiguous language that makes every product appear equally mature.

### P3 — Thesis is a navigation primitive, not slogan copy

The founder's recurring beliefs should become an inspectable section with evidence and authored notes—not marketing adjectives in the hero.

The exact Persian public label remains open, but the structural role is now justified by both audience research and reference patterns.

### P4 — Notes must prove operating judgment

Future editorial priority should be:

1. product decisions and retrospectives;
2. market observations backed by sources or internal data;
3. technical / AI implementation notes;
4. operating systems and automation lessons;
5. founder perspective on product-building in Iran and Persian-language markets.

Commodity technology news stays legacy content, not the primary editorial identity.

### P5 — Proof sits next to claims

Important claims should link to or identify the strongest available evidence:

- live product;
- repository;
- demo;
- case;
- dated metric;
- credential;
- third-party source.

This is more important than adding decorative social-proof bands.

### P6 — Progressive disclosure beats biography overload

Use a layered founder model:

- Home: identity + operating thesis + proof summary;
- About: canonical fact base + story + roles;
- Resume / Timeline: chronological evidence;
- Notes: thinking over time;
- Product pages: work as evidence.

### P7 — Conversion must follow intent

The site should not optimize for maximum contact volume. It should optimize for **qualified next actions**.

- investor / strategic conversation;
- partnership proposal;
- product-specific CTA;
- specialist collaboration;
- media / speaking inquiry.

### P8 — One canonical content graph

Founder name, roles, product names, domains, lifecycle, categories and relationships should originate from canonical registry data rather than local component mappings.

This principle is already partially implemented in the v7 branch and should become a release gate.

### P9 — Visual distinctiveness must serve recognition

The reference set shows that memorable founder sites do not require the same visual language. Distinctiveness can come from typography, density, information architecture, motion or unusual content structures.

For Amir, the design system should communicate:

- technology fluency;
- operator credibility;
- precision;
- forward motion;
- Persian editorial maturity.

It should avoid startup-template aesthetics, generic AI gradients and luxury-personal-brand styling unsupported by product evidence.

## Recommended structural consequence for Website IA v7

The landscape supports the following working top-level model:

```text
Home
├── Products
│   └── Product detail
├── Thesis
├── Notes
├── About
│   └── Resume / Timeline
└── Contact
```

Supporting surfaces:

```text
Legacy News Archive
Media / Speaking utility
Structured Product Evidence
Canonical social / GitHub links
Search / AI entity metadata
```

This is now strong enough to move from an IA hypothesis into an IA v7 design decision, subject to founder approval of audience priority and naming.

## Homepage decision hierarchy

The homepage should answer questions in this order:

1. **Identity** — Who is Amir?
2. **Thesis** — What kind of technology/product system is he building and why?
3. **Portfolio** — What are the active products?
4. **Proof** — What is verified or demonstrably live?
5. **Thinking** — What does Amir understand or believe because of operating experience?
6. **Fit** — Which path is relevant to this visitor?
7. **Action** — What should they do next?

This order is more defensible than the current biography/news/product mixture because it follows both the Audience Decision Journey and the strongest cross-reference patterns.

## Anti-patterns rejected

1. **Celebrity founder homepage** — oversized portrait, vague inspirational claim, awards and generic `Work with me` CTA.
2. **Corporate logo wall** — product logos without lifecycle, audience, founder relationship or evidence.
3. **News portal** — high-volume commodity publishing disconnected from first-hand operating expertise.
4. **Resume as homepage** — chronology before relevance.
5. **Everything-is-equal portfolio** — no distinction between active, emerging and legacy work.
6. **Investor theatre** — unsupported traction, market-size or leadership claims.
7. **AI-template aesthetic** — generic gradients, glass cards and synthetic futurism without a distinctive information system.
8. **One-contact-path conversion** — all visitors pushed to the same email link.

## Decisions now supported at Foundation level

1. The master category remains **Founder-led Technology Portfolio**.
2. `Founder → Thesis → Products → Proof` is the core information model.
3. A first-hand `Notes` layer is strategically stronger than commodity technology news.
4. Product lifecycle state and evidence state must be visible and canonical.
5. Home should summarize, not duplicate, the deep About, Product and Notes experiences.
6. Contact architecture should eventually route intent rather than expose one generic conversion.
7. Persian-first / English-useful is a differentiation opportunity, not only a translation requirement.
8. No reference site should be copied as a complete template; Amir's combined audience and portfolio problem is structurally different.
